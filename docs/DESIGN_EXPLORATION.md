# DESIGN_EXPLORATION.md

## Goal
Before major visual changes, compare multiple credible directions instead of committing to the first idea.

## Reference research
Use current, relevant references from design galleries, fashion/brand sites, editorial sites, e-commerce experiences, and licensed/free image sources. Extract design principles rather than copying a single site.

For each useful reference, capture:
- visual mood;
- composition/layout;
- typography;
- palette/contrast;
- image treatment;
- spacing/rhythm;
- motion/interaction;
- product emphasis;
- CTA/commerce behavior;
- what is reusable as a principle and what is specific to that source.

Do not reproduce distinctive layouts, branding, logos, photography, or protected creative expression from a single reference.

## A/B/C directions
For any material art-direction choice, prepare three meaningfully different directions.

### A — Commercial clarity
Prioritize product truth, legibility, confidence, clear pricing/CTA, and low-friction shopping.

### B — Brand/editorial strength
Prioritize Deadstock's refined-underground identity, stronger composition, expressive type/image rhythm, and memorable atmosphere while retaining usable commerce.

### C — Experimental edge
Explore a more distinctive layout, motion, cropping, or image system that could differentiate the brand. Keep accessibility and product truth intact.

The three options must differ in real design decisions, not just tiny color or spacing changes.

## Evaluation
Score each direction from 1–5 on:
- brand fit;
- product desire;
- product truth/trust;
- visual hierarchy;
- usability;
- mobile/iPad/desktop consistency;
- originality without imitation;
- implementation complexity;
- maintainability.

State the tradeoff and recommend one direction, but do not silently select when the decision is primarily subjective or brand-defining.

## Product-image exploration
When real product photos exist, all A/B/C directions should be grounded in the same real item. They may vary in crop, background, lighting, editorial composition, mood context, and supporting stock imagery, but must obey `docs/PRODUCT_IMAGERY.md`.

Suggested product-image exploration:
- **A:** clean real-product / premium commerce treatment;
- **B:** faithful editorial derivative with stronger lighting/composition;
- **C:** cinematic or street-context presentation using the same real product identity.

## Evidence
When implemented, generate Visual QA screenshots for phone, iPad portrait, iPad landscape, and desktop. Use the Visual Critic to compare the rendered result, not only source mockups.

## Decision rule
- If one option is objectively superior on correctness/readability/responsiveness and LOW risk, the AI may choose it.
- If multiple options are viable and differ mainly by art direction, brand tone, or taste, present A/B/C for user/owner selection.
- Never choose a direction merely because it is easiest to implement.

## Final handoff
For a design decision, report:
1. reference principles used;
2. A/B/C summary;
3. score/tradeoff table;
4. AI recommendation;
5. what changes after selection.
