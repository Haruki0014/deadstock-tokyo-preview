# PRODUCT_IMAGERY.md

## Goal
Make products feel real, desirable, and trustworthy while preserving fidelity to the actual Deadstock Tokyo item.

## Source hierarchy
Use image sources in this order:
1. actual Deadstock Tokyo product photos and owner-supplied product media;
2. faithful derivatives generated from those real product photos;
3. licensed/free-to-use mood, environment, texture, or editorial stock imagery;
4. clearly labeled concept artwork only when real product imagery is unavailable.

Never use unrelated stock clothing as if it were the actual product.

## Real product is the source of truth
Before changing a product visual, inspect the best available real photos and known product data. Preserve visible product identity, including:
- color;
- graphic/print placement;
- silhouette and proportions;
- visible seams, trims, labels, hardware, and other confirmed details.

Do not invent materials, fit, construction, features, colorways, back graphics, tags, texture, sizing, availability, or product claims that are not supported by real media or verified product data.

## When to generate a new product visual
A generated derivative is appropriate when the real photo is authentic but weak for presentation—for example poor background, weak lighting, distracting crop, insufficient product emphasis, or a mismatch with the site art direction.

Prefer the least transformative useful step:
1. crop/reframe;
2. background cleanup/replacement;
3. exposure/light/contrast cleanup;
4. faithful product isolation or editorial composition;
5. generated lifestyle/editorial derivative based on the real item.

The further the transformation goes, the stronger the fidelity review must be.

## Generated-image guardrails
Generated product imagery must remain recognizably the same real item. Before use, compare it against the source photo and reject the result if a visible product attribute has materially changed or been invented.

Generated editorial/lifestyle imagery may introduce environment, lighting, composition, styling context, or non-product props. It must not silently introduce unsupported product details.

If a model/person is generated for styling context, do not imply that the person is a real customer, ambassador, campaign participant, or owner-approved model.

## Stock and free-photo usage
Stock/free imagery is allowed for mood, streetscape, environment, texture, background, architecture, editorial atmosphere, and other contextual roles when the license permits the intended use.

Before committing a stock asset, record enough provenance to verify its origin and permitted use. Do not remove required attribution. Do not use an image when licensing is unclear.

Stock imagery must not be presented as product evidence or as photography of a Deadstock Tokyo item when it is not.

## Presentation roles
Whenever possible, give product imagery distinct jobs:
- **truth image:** clear real-item view used for confidence and verification;
- **desire image:** faithful enhanced/editorial derivative used to create desire;
- **detail image:** crop/close-up that highlights confirmed graphics or construction;
- **mood image:** contextual imagery that supports brand atmosphere without pretending to be the product.

A product section should not rely entirely on concept art when real product photography exists.

## Review checklist
Before merging a product-visual change, confirm:
- the real source item is identifiable;
- no unsupported product attribute was invented;
- the image improves clarity, desire, or brand fit;
- real/derived/stock/concept roles are not misleading;
- image source/license provenance is known where applicable;
- mobile, iPad, and desktop crops remain effective;
- Visual QA passes;
- Visual Critic has reviewed the latest screenshots.

## Escalate
Ask for user/owner direction instead of silently deciding when:
- the source photos conflict or are too unclear to preserve product identity;
- the proposed generation would materially reinterpret the item;
- the change affects official campaign imagery or public brand claims;
- several art directions are equally plausible.

For several valid directions, use the A/B/C workflow in `docs/DESIGN_EXPLORATION.md`.
